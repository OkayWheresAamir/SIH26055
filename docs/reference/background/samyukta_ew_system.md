The Samyukta Electronic Warfare (EW) system operates primarily in an Electronic Support Measures (ESM) capacity. It utilizes a wide array of highly specialized passive radio frequency (RF) and electromagnetic sensors rather than optical or infrared sensors.

Because it is an electronic warfare system, its passive sensors do not emit energy; instead, they stealthily detect, intercept, search, identify, and log enemy emissions across a massive bandwidth ranging from 1.5 MHz to 40 GHz (High Frequency to Millimeter Wave bands).

The system organizes these passive sensors into two distinct segments:

1. Communications Intelligence (COMINT) Sensors

These passive sensors are dedicated to intercepting and monitoring enemy communication channels. They include:

Direction Finding (DF) Sensors: Determine the exact line of bearing or angle of arrival of an enemy transmission.

Position Fixing / Geolocation Sensors: Cross-reference multiple passive signal receptions across vehicles to triangulate and lock onto the precise coordinates of adversary radio transmitters.

Signal Intercept and Analysis Receivers: Wideband passive antennas and receivers that scan, capture, and decode complex tactical signals, including military radios, wideband radio relays, cellular data, satellite transmissions, and UAV signals.

2. Electronic Intelligence (ELINT) / Non-Communication Sensors

These passive sensors are tuned specifically to pick up non-communications signals, mostly radar footprints. They include:

Radar Warning / ESM Receivers: Highly sensitive passive sensors that scan the environment for hostile ground-based and airborne radar pulses.

Pulse Parameter and Digital Signal Processors: Passive sensors equipped to accurately measure specific incoming temporal wave traits, such as pulse duration, pulsed chirp modulation, phase-shift keying, and pulse repetition intervals.

Frequency Determination Sensors: Instantly capture the exact microwave frequency (across X-band, Ku-band, etc.) of an active enemy radar to cross-reference it against an internal database of radar fingerprints.
